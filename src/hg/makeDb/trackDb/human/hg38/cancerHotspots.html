Description

This track is part of the Cancer Mutations collection. It shows the mutation hotspots from cancerhotspots.org: single amino acids, short stretches of amino acids or splice sites of a protein that are mutated in tumors much more often than expected from the background mutation rate of the gene. Most of these are driver mutations that are selected for during tumor growth, including well-known ones such as KRAS G12, BRAF V600 or IDH1 R132, but most of them are rare and are found in fewer than 1 in 1000 patients.

The track contains 1328 hotspots from two releases. Release v2 (Chang et al. 2017) has about 1100 single-residue and splice-site hotspots and 55 in-frame insertion and deletion hotspots, found in 24,592 tumors. Release v3 (Bandlamudi et al. 2026) adds 164 new single-residue hotspots, found in about 45,000 tumors sequenced with MSK-IMPACT.

Display Conventions and Configuration

Each hotspot is drawn over its codon, or over the codons of the amino acid range for in-frame indel hotspots. Codons that are split by an intron are drawn as two blocks. Splice-site hotspots are drawn as one block for each mutated nucleotide. The item label is the gene and the residue as given by the source, e.g. "KRAS G12" or "EGFR 745-759".

The mouseover shows the type, the number of tumors with a mutation at the hotspot, the q-value and the three cancer tissues with the most mutated tumors. The details page lists all mutations at the hotspot with their tumor counts, all cancer tissues, whether the hotspot is annotated in OncoKB and, for v3 hotspots, the frequency in the MSK-IMPACT, other AACR GENIE and TCGA cohorts. Items can be filtered by the number of tumors, the hotspot type, the release, OncoKB annotation and cancer tissue.

Methods

Release v2 (Chang et al. 2017) is based on somatic mutations of 24,592 tumors from 322 cancer types: 14,256 mostly primary tumors from TCGA, ICGC and published studies, and 10,945 tumors of 10,336 patients with advanced disease, sequenced with the targeted MSK-IMPACT panel. The statistical significance of each codon was computed separately for 32 organ types and for the whole cohort, with a truncated binomial model whose background rate takes into account the gene length, the gene- and position-specific mutability (trinucleotide context per organ type) and the overall mutation count of the gene. In-frame indels were grouped into regions of overlapping indels and tested with the same model, without the mutability terms. Release v3 (Bandlamudi et al. 2026) applied the same approach to 45,492 MSK-IMPACT tumors from 77 cancer types. Of 313 candidate codons with q < 0.01 and at least five mutated tumors, 164 remained after removing candidates in tumors with very high mutation burden, in low-complexity sequence, with mostly subclonal mutations, or whose cancer types did not match those of the known hotspots in the same gene.

We downloaded the files hotspots_v2.xls and hotspots_v3.xlsx from the cancerhotspots.org download page, as well as the v2 mutation file cancerhotspots.v2.maf.gz. The v3 file contains the 164 new hotspots; all other hotspots are from the v2 file. The source gives hotspots as a gene and an amino acid number, so we placed each one on an Ensembl 115 transcript and required the reference amino acid(s) of the source to match the translated transcript. For v2 hotspots, the source also lists the hg19 positions of the mutations at each hotspot. We lifted them to hg38 and picked the transcript whose codon(s) contain most of them. The v2 residue numbers refer to Ensembl 75 transcripts, and for 9 hotspots in MYD88, FGFR1 and ARID1B no current transcript has the reference amino acid at that number. For these, the residue number on the current transcript is shown on the details page, e.g. MYD88 L265 is L252 on the current MANE transcript. For v3 hotspots, we used the transcript picked for the v2 hotspots of the same gene, or the Ensembl canonical transcript. Splice-site hotspots have no codon and are drawn at the lifted hg19 mutation positions. One of the 1329 hotspots is not in the track: the only hg19 position of the splice-site hotspot RECQL4 X766 could not be lifted to hg38. The scripts are in the kent source tree, and all steps are described in the makeDoc file.

Data Access

The data can be explored interactively in table format with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator and exported from there to spreadsheet or tab-separated tables. From scripts, the data can be accessed through our API, track=cancerHotspots.

For automated download and analysis, the annotation is stored in a bigBed file that can be downloaded from our download server. The file for this track is called cancerHotspots.bb. Individual regions or the whole genome annotation can be obtained using our tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a precompiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain features within a given range, e.g. bigBedToBed https://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/hg38/cancerMutations/cancerHotspots.bb -chrom=chr12 -start=25000000 -end=26000000 stdout

The original data can be downloaded from cancerhotspots.org. It is available under the ODC Open Database License (ODbL). The source code of the hotspot method is on GitHub.
