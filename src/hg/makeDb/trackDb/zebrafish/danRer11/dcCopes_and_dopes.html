Description

Most candidate regulatory elements can be recognized by the histone modifications around them: promoters carry H3K4me3, enhancers carry H3K4me1 and H3K27ac. The DANIO-CODE consortium found a substantial number of regions that are clearly open, and therefore bound by something, but that carry no histone mark at any developmental stage. They called these orphan elements, and split them in two by how their accessibility behaves over time.

COPEs, constitutive orphan predicted elements, stay open throughout development. There are 2,202 of them, and about 40% contain a CTCF binding motif, which suggests that many of them are structural rather than regulatory. DOPEs, dynamic orphan predicted elements, are open only at particular stages. The pooled set shown here has 10,720 elements. Only a handful of DOPEs overlap a CAGE promoter, but a quarter of them pick up active chromatin marks later, in adult tissues, while already being as open as an active enhancer in the embryo. What they do during embryogenesis is not settled.

This track is part of the DANIO-CODE collection.

Display Conventions and Configuration

The two sets are separate tracks and are drawn in the colors stored in the data files. The per-stage DOPE annotation, which keeps the stages apart instead of pooling them, is in the DC Elements track.

Methods

The DANIO-CODE consortium assembled 1,802 zebrafish developmental genomics datasets, 1,438 of them already published and 366 generated by consortium members, and reprocessed all of them from the raw sequencing reads so that samples from different laboratories and different protocols can be compared with each other. ChIP-seq and ATAC-seq were run through the ENCODE pipelines, CAGE-seq through the FANTOM pipeline, and Hi-C and 4C-seq through the pipelines of the groups that produced them. The pipelines are published at gitlab.com/danio-code, and samples were assigned to developmental stages using ZFIN and ENCODE nomenclature. See Baranasic et al. 2022 for details.

Candidate regulatory elements without any detectable histone modification at any stage were separated by their openness profile across development: those open at all stages became COPEs, those open only at some stages became DOPEs. CTCF motif content and overlap with adult chromatin marks were assessed afterwards. See Baranasic et al. 2022 for the criteria.

At UCSC the tracks were converted from the consortium's public track hub at trackhub2.genereg.net/DANIO-CODE with the script danioCodeHubToRa.py, and the data files were copied from the same server. The data themselves were not modified. The steps are documented in our makeDoc.

Data Access

The data can be explored interactively in table format with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator and exported from there to spreadsheet or tab-separated tables. From scripts, the data can be accessed through our API, track=dcCopes_and_dopes.

For automated download and analysis, the annotations are stored in bigBed files that can be downloaded from our download server. The files are copes_dr11.bb and dopes_all_dr11.bb. Individual regions or the whole genome annotation can be obtained using our tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a precompiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain features within a given range, for example 

bigBedToBed http://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/danRer11/danioCode/copes_dr11.bb \
    -chrom=chr1 -start=20000000 -end=20100000 stdout

The original data files, and the sample and protocol metadata behind them, are available from the DANIO-CODE data coordination center at danio-code.zfin.org and from the consortium's track hub at trackhub2.genereg.net/DANIO-CODE.
