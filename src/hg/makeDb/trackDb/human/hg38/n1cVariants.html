Description

This track is part of the Drug Targets collection. It shows about 500 disease-causing variants in 98 genes that were assessed for eligibility for a variant-specific antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) therapy, from the Assessed Variants section of the N1C Gene Registry.

Each variant is classified for one therapeutic approach, usually the one that fits the variant best: splice correction (an ASO blocks a splice site created by the variant), exon skipping (an ASO removes the exon that contains the variant, which only helps if the shorter protein still works), or knockdown (an ASO or siRNA lowers the amount of a toxic or dominant-negative transcript). The classification says whether the variant is a molecular candidate for such a therapy. It does not mean that a therapy exists or that one can be developed for a patient.

Display Conventions and Configuration

Each item is one assessment, labeled with the gene and the coding DNA change. A variant that was assessed more than once is shown once per assessment. Deletions and duplications cover the deleted or duplicated bases, insertions cover the two bases around the insertion. Items are colored by the eligibility class:

  Eligible
  Likely eligible
  Unlikely eligible
  Not eligible
  Unable to assess

Hover over an item to see the variant, its eligibility and the approach. Click it for the full assessment text, links to ClinVar, the MONDO disease entry and publications, and a link to the entry in the N1C Gene Registry. The track can be filtered by eligibility, approach, inheritance and the group that did the assessment on the track configuration page.

Methods

The assessments follow the N1C VARIANT guidelines (Cheerie et al. 2025), a consensus of clinicians, geneticists and ASO developers in the N=1 Collaborative. The guidelines apply to (likely) pathogenic variants and assess them separately for splice correction, exon skipping and transcript knockdown, based on the variant type and its position in the transcript, the reading frame and protein domains of the exon, the pathomechanism of the disease (loss of function, gain of function, dominant negative) and whether the gene is dosage sensitive. Each variant is classified as eligible, likely eligible, unlikely eligible or not eligible for an approach, or as unable to assess. Most assessments in the registry were done by trained volunteer assessors of the N=1 Collaborative; others were submitted by GeneSteps, UPNAT and the Yu lab at Boston Children's Hospital. See the reference below for details.

The data was downloaded from the N1C Gene Registry API at https://gene-registry.onrender.com/api/data?table=assessed_variants. The table had 2,660 rows, of which 2,145 were blank, leaving 515 assessments, all of which are in the track. 508 were placed using their GRCh38 genomic HGVS. The genomic HGVS of 507 of them was compared with the position of their RefSeq transcript HGVS mapped with our tool hgvsToVcf and none disagreed; the reference base of all 373 substitutions matched hg38. Seven variants had no genomic HGVS and were placed by mapping the transcript HGVS with hgvsToVcf: a deletion of exon 4 of DYSF (twice), two AP4M1 variants, and three CAG repeat expansions in ATN1 and ATXN7, which are shown on the first base of the repeat. The separate chromosome, position and allele columns of the registry were not used, as they disagree with the HGVS for 22 variants. Spellings of the eligibility, approach, inheritance and other categories were normalized, e.g. "Not eligiblle" to "Not eligible", and one assessment marked as "Unable to assess/Not Eligible" is shown as "Unable to assess". The commands are documented in our makeDoc file and the scripts are in our source repository.

Data Access

The data can be explored interactively in table format with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator and exported from there to spreadsheet or tab-separated tables. From scripts, the data can be accessed through our API, track=n1cVariants.

For automated download and analysis, the annotation is stored in a bigBed file that can be downloaded from our download server. The file for this track is called n1cVariants.bb. Individual regions or the whole annotation can be obtained using our tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a precompiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain features within a given range, e.g. bigBedToBed http://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/hg38/drugTargets/n1cVariants.bb -chrom=chr11 -start=0 -end=10000000 stdout

The original data can be downloaded from the N1C Gene Registry API. The registry data is free for nonprofit, academic and research use.
