Description

Part of the Imprinting track collection, this track shows 50 imprinted differentially methylated regions (DMRs) that Monk et al. (2018) proposed as the standard set for diagnostic testing of imprinting disorders, annotated with the clinical classification from the more recent joint statement on multi-locus imprinting disturbance (MLID) by Mackay et al. (2024). The two papers list the same regions with identical hg38 coordinates, except two intronic DMRs at SNRPN that only Monk et al. list.

Each region has a systematic name built from the gene, the position relative to it (promoter, exon, intron or intergenic) and the type of region, for example H19/IGF2:IG-DMR. The older names under which a region is commonly known, such as ICR1 or KvDMR1, are listed as other names. For each region the track gives the parent whose allele carries the methylation, the number of CpG sites, whether the methylation is established in the oocyte or in the sperm (germline DMR) or only after fertilization (secondary DMR), and the matching Locus Reference Genomic (LRG) record where one exists.

Mackay et al. classify each region by whether methylation there is used in diagnosis. A clinically associated DMR is one where a change in methylation is directly associated with an imprinting disorder, or is used as a proxy to detect imprinting disturbance. A non-clinical DMR is not currently associated with a clinical phenotype or used in diagnosis. For the clinically associated regions the track gives the imprinting disorders in which they are involved.

Display Conventions and Configuration

The color shows the parent whose allele is methylated, and the shade shows the classification:

  Red: clinically associated DMR, maternal allele methylated
  Blue: clinically associated DMR, paternal allele methylated
  Light red: non-clinical DMR or not classified by Mackay et al., maternal allele methylated
  Light blue: non-clinical DMR, paternal allele methylated

The track can be filtered by clinical classification, methylated allele and imprint origin. The details page also shows the type given in Table 1 of Mackay et al. (clinically associated, non-clinical, or variable methylation that cannot be interpreted clinically), whether the region is marked as the key diagnostic DMR of its locus, which of the two papers list it, and the position as published by Monk et al. in hg19.

Methods

Monk et al. defined the extent of each DMR from methyl-seq data sets and named the regions with the nomenclature proposed by EUCID, using HGNC-approved gene names, so that the same region is reported under one name in the clinic. Mackay et al. list the regions with their hg38 location, number of CpG sites, imprint origin, associated disorders and type. Their expert working groups based the classification on current knowledge and on DNA from blood, the most frequently used diagnostic tissue. Tissue-specific, transient and polymorphically imprinted loci are not part of the classification. See the papers for details.

The Monk et al. table was copied from Table 1 of the paper on PubMed Central. Starts in the table are 1-based, so one was subtracted to get the BED start, and the regions were lifted from hg19 to hg38 with liftOver. All 50 regions were converted. The thousands separators the web page inserted into the LRG identifiers were removed, the dash and prime characters in two names were converted to ASCII, and M and P were spelled out as Maternal and Paternal. The coordinates of SNRPN:Int1-DMR1 are shown as published, 100 kb long for 44 CpGs, which is probably a typing error in the paper.

The Mackay et al. tables were copied from PubMed Central (Table 1, Table 2, Table 3). Its 48 regions have exactly the same hg38 coordinates as 48 of the lifted Monk et al. regions, and were joined on them. The H19/IGF2 region is listed in Table 1 as one row for the IG-DMR and the TSS-DMR and was matched to the IG-DMR. Table 1 does not state which parent's allele is methylated: for regions with an oocyte germline origin the maternal allele was taken, for a sperm germline origin the paternal allele, and for the nine regions with only a secondary origin, the parent given by Monk et al.; it agrees with Monk et al. for all 48 regions. The clinical classification comes from Table 3. Six regions are not in Table 3: five that Table 1 marks as variable methylation (GPR1-AS:TSS-DMR, VTRNA2-1:DMR, WDR27:Int13-DMR, INPP5F:Int2-DMR, MKRN3:TSS-DMR) are shown as non-clinical, and IGF2:Ex9-DMR, which Table 1 marks as clinically associated, is shown as clinically associated. Two regions that Table 1 marks as clinically associated (IGF2:alt-TSS-DMR and MEG3:TSS-DMR) are listed as non-clinical in Table 3, and the track follows Table 3. The disorder acronyms of Table 1 were expanded using Table 2; upd(7)mat was expanded as maternal uniparental disomy of chromosome 7, which causes Silver-Russell syndrome, and upd(20)mat as maternal uniparental disomy of chromosome 20, connected here with Mulchandani-Bhoj-Conlin syndrome, the only chromosome 20 syndrome in Table 2.

Where the two papers differ, the track shows the names of Monk et al. and the other values of Mackay et al. Two names differ in spelling (Monk ZNF597:3' DMR and GNAS A/B:TSS-DMR, Mackay ZNF597:3-DMR and GNAS-A/B:TSS-DMR); the Mackay spelling is listed as another name. The CpG counts differ for three regions, DIRAS3:Ex2-DMR, DIRAS3:TSS-DMR (whose counts are swapped between the papers) and SVOPL:alt-TSS-DMR, and both numbers are shown. Monk et al. call the secondary DMRs "No-secondary DMR", which is shown as "Secondary DMR" as in Mackay et al. The two SNRPN regions that Mackay et al. do not list are classified as "Not in Mackay 2024". See the makeDoc file and the scripts in monk2018Imprint and mackay2024Imprint.

Data Access

The data can be explored interactively in table format with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator and exported from there to spreadsheet or tab-sep tables. From scripts, the data can be accessed through our API, track=monk2018Imprint.

For automated download and analysis, the genome annotation is stored in a bigBed file that can be downloaded from our download server. The file for this track is called monk2018Imprint.bb. Individual regions or the whole genome annotation can be obtained using our tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a precompiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain features within a given range, e.g. bigBedToBed http://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/hg38/imprinting/monk2018/monk2018Imprint.bb -chrom=chr11 -start=0 -end=3000000 stdout

The original data can be viewed in Table 1 of Monk et al. and Table 1 of Mackay et al.
