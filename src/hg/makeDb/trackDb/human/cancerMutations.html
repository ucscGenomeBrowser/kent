Description

Tumors acquire somatic mutations that are not present in the germline of the patient. Counting how often the same mutation is found in patients with a given cancer type helps to separate recurrent driver mutations from rare passenger mutations.

This collection contains tracks with somatic mutations found by sequencing tumor samples:

  • Cancer Hotspots: more than 1300 amino acids, amino acid ranges and splice sites that are mutated in tumors more often than expected by chance, from cancerhotspots.org.
  • IntOGen Drivers: 633 genes whose mutations show signs of positive selection in tumors, found in 271 sequencing cohorts by IntOGen, with their mode of action and the cohorts and cancer types where they are drivers.
  • Cancer Mutation Atlas (hg38 only): 3.9 million somatic variants from 12 sequencing studies, with the number of patients carrying each variant and the number of patients tested, for more than 800 OncoTree cancer types.
  • CMA Lollipops (hg38 only): the same data, drawn as a lollipop plot. The height of a lollipop is the log10 of the number of carriers.
  • TCGA PanCan33 and TCGA Breast (hg38 only): somatic mutations of The Cancer Genome Atlas, see below.

TCGA PanCan33 and TCGA Breast

These two tracks show the somatic mutations of the 33 TCGA cancer projects, as called by MuTect2 and released in the NCI Genomic Data Commons (GDC) data release 10.0 (January 2019). TCGA PanCan33 contains about 2.9 million variants of all 33 projects, TCGA Breast the 120,000 variants of the breast cancer project TCGA-BRCA. Each item is one variant, labeled with its reference and alternative allele. The details page shows the number of samples with the variant, the gene and variant class, the TCGA projects, and per sample the clinical data of the GDC (sex, ethnicity, smoking and alcohol history, days to death) with a link to the GDC case page. The MAF files were converted with the script cancerMafToBigBed in the kent source tree. The TCGA Pan-Cancer track shows the same release as one lollipop track per project.

Data Access

The documentation pages of the individual tracks describe how to download the data and how to intersect it with other annotations. The TCGA tracks can be exported with the Table Browser; the original MAF files are available from the GDC.
