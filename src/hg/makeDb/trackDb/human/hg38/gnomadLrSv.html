Description

This track is part of the gnomAD collection. It shows structural variants (SVs) of 50 bp or more from the first long-read release of gnomAD (version 0.1, October 2026). The variants were called from PacBio HiFi genome sequencing of 1,319 individuals in two cohorts:

  • All of Us: 1,027 participants of the All of Us Research Program who self-identify as Black or African American, sequenced to about 8x coverage from blood DNA.
  • HPRC/HGSVC: 292 publicly available samples of the Human Pangenome Reference Consortium and the Human Genome Structural Variation Consortium, sequenced to more than 30x from cell lines.

gnomAD shows the two cohorts separately. Here they are merged into one track: a variant found in both cohorts with exactly the same alleles is shown once, with the allele counts of both cohorts. The track contains 1.1 million structural variants: 250,000 deletions, 790,000 insertions and 18,000 tandem duplications. The long-read tandem repeat calls of the same release are in the gnomAD LR TRs track.

Display Conventions

Deletions are drawn over the deleted bases. Insertions are drawn as a 1-bp feature on the base before the insertion point. Tandem duplications are drawn over the reference segment that was duplicated. Items are labeled with the SV type and length, e.g. DEL-3.9k. By default, items are colored by SV type, with the same colors as the Long-read SVs tracks:

  Deletion (DEL)
  Insertion (INS), including mobile element insertions
  Tandem duplication (DUP)

On the track settings page, "Color by" can be switched to "Allele frequency". The items are then colored on a scale from light gray to red, by the allele frequency over both cohorts. The scale is logarithmic: an allele frequency of 0.0001 or less is light gray, 0.001 is pink, 0.01 is light red, 0.1 is red and 1 is dark red. This is similar to the haplotype-count color scale of the gnomAD browser, but it is based on allele frequency.

  AF 0.0001 or lower
  AF 0.001
  AF 0.01
  AF 0.1
  AF 1

The mouseover shows the type and length, the allele frequency over both cohorts and the allele frequency and count in each cohort, the VCF filter if it is not PASS, and the predicted gene effects. The details page has links to the variant in the gnomAD long-read browser, one per cohort.

The track can be filtered by SV type, length, allele frequency and count, cohort, VCF filter value and genomic context. No filter is active by default, so the track also shows variants that gnomAD did not pass:

  • SINGLE_READ_SUPPORT: the only carrier has a single supporting read.
  • ASSEMBLY_ONLY_SINGLETON: the only carrier has support only from the assembly-based callers (HPRC/HGSVC only).
  • LOW_COVERAGE_REGION: the region has low coverage relative to the genome.
  • MONOALLELIC: the variant is in a region with several variants that could not be merged into non-overlapping multi-allelic sites.

To show only variants that passed all filters, select only "PASS" in the VCF filter list. When zoomed out to more than 1 Mbp, the track is shown as a density graph.

Methods

All of Us samples were sequenced with PacBio HiFi to about 8x and processed with the All of Us Long Read Working Group pipeline. HPRC/HGSVC samples were sequenced to more than 30x. Their SVs come from the HPRC SV integration pipeline, which combines read-based callers with the assembly-based callers dipcall and hapdiff, followed by regenotyping with kanpig. SVs were annotated with GATK SVAnnotate for their effect on genes, and with their matches to gnomAD v4 short-read variants. Variants were reclassified, e.g. tandem duplications that are not adjacent to their origin were relabeled as insertions, and mobile element insertions and deletions were labeled with their subfamily. The pipelines are on GitHub, and the release is described in the gnomAD blog post.

The per-chromosome VCF files of both cohorts were downloaded from the gnomAD Amazon S3 bucket, s3://gnomad-public-us-east-1/release/lr/0.1/vcf/, which is listed on the gnomAD long-read downloads page. All records with allele_type del, ins or dup and an allele_length of at least 50 bp were kept, whatever their source (SV caller or DeepVariant) and FILTER value. The records of the two cohorts were merged when position, REF and ALT were identical. The allele frequency of the merged item is the sum of the allele counts of both cohorts, divided by the sum of their allele numbers. The input had 855,161 All of Us and 331,729 HPRC/HGSVC records; 129,535 alleles were found in both cohorts, which gives 1,057,355 items, 908,010 of them with FILTER PASS in all cohorts that have them. These numbers include variants that did not pass the gnomAD filters, so they are higher than the SV numbers in the gnomAD announcement. 539 variants have an allele number of 0 (no sample was genotyped); their allele frequency is 0. The commands are in the makeDoc file and the scripts in the gnomadLr scripts directory.

Data Access

The data can be explored interactively in table format with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator and exported from there to spreadsheet or tab-separated tables. From scripts, the data can be accessed through our API, track=gnomadLrSv.

For automated download and analysis, the annotation is stored in a bigBed file that can be downloaded from our download server. The file for this track is called gnomadLrSv.bb. Individual regions or the whole annotation can be obtained using our tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a precompiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain features within a given range, e.g. bigBedToBed http://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/hg38/gnomAD/lr/gnomadLrSv.bb -chrom=chr21 -start=0 -end=100000000 stdout

The original VCF files, which also contain SNVs and small indels and, for HPRC/HGSVC, the sample genotypes, can be downloaded from the gnomAD long-read downloads page. gnomAD data is released without use restrictions.
