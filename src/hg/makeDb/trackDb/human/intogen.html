Description

This track is part of the Cancer Mutations collection. It shows the mutational cancer driver genes of IntOGen: genes whose somatic point mutations and short indels show signs of positive selection in tumors, found by running seven driver discovery methods on the mutations of each tumor cohort. The current release analyzed 271 cohorts with more than 33,000 tumors and lists 633 driver genes. Each gene is drawn once, on the Ensembl transcript that IntOGen used, and the details page lists all cohorts in which it was found to be a driver.

Display Conventions and Configuration

Genes are colored by their mode of action, the role that was most often assigned to the gene over all cohorts in which it is a driver:

  Activating (oncogene)
  Loss of function (tumor suppressor)
  Ambiguous

The mouseover shows the mode of action, the number of cohorts and cancer types in which the gene is a driver, and the three cancer types with the highest fraction of mutated tumors. The details page has a table of all these cohorts, with the cancer type, the data source, the number and fraction of mutated tumors, the combined q-value, the mode of action and the methods that found the gene. A second table lists the regions of the protein where mutations cluster: significant Pfam domains, linear clusters and 3D clusters, with amino acid positions. Genes can be filtered by mode of action, number of cohorts, highest fraction of mutated tumors in a cohort and cancer type.

Methods

IntOGen (Martinez-Jimenez et al. 2020) collects somatic mutations from whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing studies, among them TCGA, PCAWG, the Hartwig Medical Foundation, St. Jude, CPTAC and many published cohorts of primary, metastatic and relapsed tumors. Before the analysis, mutations are lifted to GRCh38 and filtered for low mappability, multiple samples of the same donor are reduced to one, and hypermutated samples are removed. In each cohort, seven methods look for different signals of positive selection: an excess of non-synonymous mutations (dNdScv, CBaSE), a bias towards functionally important mutations (OncodriveFML), clustering of mutations along the protein sequence (OncodriveCLUSTL), in protein domains (smRegions) or in the 3D structure (HotMAPS), and a combination of mutation rate and clustering (MutPanning). The p-values of the methods are combined with a weighted Stouffer Z-score. The weight of each method is estimated per cohort from how well its ranking agrees with the known driver genes of the Cancer Gene Census. Genes with a combined q-value below 0.05 that are significant in at least two methods, and known cancer genes with weaker evidence, were then filtered: genes not expressed in the tumor type, genes that are tolerant to variation in the population, likely artifacts such as very long genes or olfactory receptors, genes with more than two mutations in one sample and genes targeted by local hypermutation were removed. The mode of action was inferred from the distribution of mutations of a pan-cancer dNdScv run and combined with the literature, as collected by the Cancer Genome Interpreter.

We downloaded the driver gene catalog and the cohort table of release 2024.09.20, IntOGen-Drivers-20240920.zip and IntOGen-Cohorts-20240920.zip, from the IntOGen download page. The file Compendium_Cancer_Genes.tsv has 4478 rows, one per gene and cohort, for 633 genes in 271 cohorts. We merged the rows of each gene into one item and drew it on the Ensembl transcript given by IntOGen, using the gene models of Ensembl release 115. The mode of action of a gene on the browser is the one most often assigned in its cohorts. For hg19, the hg38 items were converted with liftOver. CLTCL1 and CRLF2 could not be converted and were drawn on the same transcript from Ensembl GRCh37 release 87. The scripts are in the kent source tree, and all steps are described in the makeDoc file.

Data Access

The data can be explored interactively in table format with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator and exported from there to spreadsheet or tab-separated tables. From scripts, the data can be accessed through our API, track=intogen.

For automated download and analysis, the annotation is stored in a bigBed file that can be downloaded from our download server (replace hg38 with hg19 in the URL for hg19). The file for this track is called intogen.bb. Individual regions or the whole genome annotation can be obtained using our tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a precompiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain features within a given range, e.g. bigBedToBed https://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/hg38/cancerMutations/intogen.bb -chrom=chr12 -start=25000000 -end=26000000 stdout

The original data can be downloaded from intogen.org. It is available under the CC0 1.0 public domain dedication. The pipeline is described in the IntOGen documentation.
