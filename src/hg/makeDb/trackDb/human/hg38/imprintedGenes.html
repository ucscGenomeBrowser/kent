Description

Part of the Imprinting track collection, this track shows the genes that two hand-curated catalogs list as imprinted or candidate imprinted: OMIM, the catalog of human genes and genetic disorders curated at Johns Hopkins, and Geneimprint, a catalog of imprinted genes maintained by Randy L. Jirtle. A gene that is in both catalogs is shown twice, once for each source.

The OMIM part has 459 loci. OMIM marks a gene as imprinted or a candidate by reading the primary literature, and publishes this mark only through GeneScout, OMIM's tool for listing the genes and phenotypes inside a set of genomic intervals, which appends (I) to the location of a flagged gene. The mark is not in the OMIM gene map or in any OMIM download file. The OMIM curators point out that it covers both genes where imprinting is established and genes where it is only a candidate, and that the export does not say which is which, so an OMIM gene in this track is a pointer into its OMIM entry rather than a settled call. 225 of the loci have an OMIM gene entry of their own; the other 234 are in the gene map without one and are mostly antisense transcripts, long non-coding RNAs and microRNAs inside imprinted clusters, such as KCNQ1-AS1 and INS-IGF2.

The Geneimprint part has 274 genes, the whole human catalog. The catalog gives only a cytogenetic band, so the coordinates come from resolving the gene symbols against the HGNC gene annotation. It includes 99 genes that are only predicted to be imprinted, 18 that Geneimprint records as not imprinted and 10 whose status is unknown. Use the filters on the track configuration page to narrow the display to the sources and evidence levels you want.

Display Conventions and Configuration

Each item is one gene, drawn over the gene span given by OMIM or over the resolved HGNC locus for Geneimprint. OMIM does not report a strand, so OMIM items are drawn without one. Geneimprint items are labeled with the name Geneimprint uses, which for older entries can differ from today's approved symbol. The color shows the source:

  Green: OMIM
  Blue: Geneimprint, all statuses except Predicted
  Gray: Geneimprint, status Predicted

Clicking an item opens a page with the details of its source. For Geneimprint these are the imprint status, the expressed parental allele, the band given by the catalog and links to the Geneimprint and HGNC entries. For OMIM they are the MIM number with a link to the OMIM entry, the full gene name and every OMIM phenotype associated with the gene, with its mode of inheritance.

Filters are available for the source, the Geneimprint imprint status and expressed allele, and whether an OMIM locus has a gene entry of its own. None of them filter anything out by default. The Geneimprint filters remove the OMIM genes when they are used, and the OMIM filter removes the Geneimprint genes.

Methods

OMIM curators read the primary literature and record, for each gene, the phenotypes it causes, the mode of inheritance and a set of annotations, one of which is that the gene is imprinted or a candidate for it. GeneScout is described in Applegate et al. (2022). Geneimprint is curated by hand from the imprinting literature. A large share of its entries marked as predicted rather than confirmed comes from the genome-wide machine learning screen of Luedi et al. (2007), which scored every human gene on the sequence features that distinguish known imprinted genes, mainly the arrangement of repeats and transcription factor binding sites around the locus. The catalog records for each gene the expressed parental allele and an evidence level: imprinted, not imprinted, predicted, conflicting data, provisional data, tissue dependent or unknown. The site does not publish a definition of these levels.

The OMIM gene list was exported from GeneScout as a tab-delimited file, using a search that covers every chromosome on assembly GRCh38, and the flagged entries were converted to browser coordinates. GeneScout also lists OMIM phenotype entries alongside genes; those are mapped disease regions rather than gene positions, some of them tens of megabases long, so 108 of them were not included. The Genome Browser already has them in the OMIM Cyto Loci track. The Geneimprint catalog table was downloaded from https://geneimprint.com/site/genes-by-species.Homo+sapiens and parsed out of the HTML page. Each gene symbol was resolved against the HGNC track, trying in turn the approved symbol, the previous approved symbols and the alias symbols, first for the catalog name and then for each of the aliases the catalog lists. Only the main chromosomes were considered. Where a symbol matched more than one locus, the one whose cytogenetic band agreed with the catalog was taken. Two cluster names, SNORD115@ and SNORD116, do not exist as single loci and were spanned from the first to the last numbered member of their family. One symbol that HGNC has retired, PRR25, was resolved through RefSeq instead. The catalog lists 281 entries: four could not be placed because their gene identifiers have been retired (one of those, PWCR1, is an older name for the SNORD116 cluster, which is in the track under that name), and three genes appear twice under an old and a current symbol and are drawn once, carrying both names. The two gene lists were then merged into one file. The processing steps are documented in the imprinting makeDoc and the scripts are in makeDb/scripts/imprinting.

Data Access

The data can be explored interactively in table format with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator and exported from there to spreadsheet or tab-sep tables. From scripts, the data can be accessed through our API, track=imprintedGenes.

For automated download and analysis, the genome annotation is stored in a bigBed file that can be downloaded from our download server. The file for this track is called imprintedGenes.bb. Individual regions or the whole genome annotation can be obtained using our tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a precompiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain features within a given range, e.g. bigBedToBed http://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/hg38/imprinting/imprintedGenes/imprintedGenes.bb -chrom=chr15 -start=23000000 -end=26000000 stdout

The original OMIM gene list can be exported from GeneScout. Use of OMIM resources requires agreement to the OMIM terms of use. The original Geneimprint catalog can be browsed at https://geneimprint.com/site/genes-by-species.Homo+sapiens.
